Key Themes & Takeaways

What the session surfaced

Six through-lines that ran across the demos, Q&A, and the extended conversation after the official end.

🏎️

The Ferrari in Your Garage

Most nonprofits already have a fully licensed Google Workspace for Nonprofits — and the powerful AI baked into it — and aren't using it. The whole session was an answer to one question: what's already there?

📓

NotebookLM Is the Underrated Star

Joshua called it "the most criminally underused AI tool out there." It's a grounded RAG engine — it draws only from your documents, not the open web — and it generates audio podcasts, slide decks, mind maps, and video scripts, all from your own org's knowledge.

🔗

It's One Workflow, Not Six Tools

The power isn't any single tool — it's that they talk to each other. Tuesday's AI policy grounds the chatbot, the grant application, and the board deck. Monday's sign-up form feeds Thursday's board numbers. One organization's knowledge, flowing.

🔒

Your Data Stays Yours

A question that dominated the chat: Is our data safe? Kelsey (Google for Nonprofits) answered directly: when you're signed into Workspace for Nonprofits, your inputs to Gemini and NotebookLM are not used to train Google's models — the same enterprise protections that cover your Drive and Gmail apply.

⏱️

The Cost of Doing Nothing

A website with a volunteer form, a Google Sheet integration, an automated follow-up email, and an AI chatbot: Joshua estimated that would have been a $20,000, six-month project three years ago. That's now about an hour, if you know what you're doing. The gap between "we know tools exist" and "we actually use them" is costing organizations real money.

🌍

The Equity Gap in AI Language

One of the most important moments in the session: an attendee raised the question of how well these tools perform for organizations working in non-English languages, including nuanced multilingual content. Kelsey shared Google's Responsible AI Impact Lab resources. Joshua acknowledged it as "a hard problem" and committed to following up. This conversation isn't over.

Open Questions

Questions raised in the session that are worth sitting with — some answered, some still open.

How well do these tools actually work in non-English languages?
A real limitation. Accented text, multilingual RAG retrieval, and training data bias toward English are genuine issues. Google's Responsible AI Impact Lab is one resource; this is an active area of research. If your org works multilingually, test before you commit.
What's the real difference between Gemini and NotebookLM?
Think of NotebookLM as a harness: same underlying AI, but built specifically for grounded retrieval from your documents. Gemini is more expansive — the open web, your Drive broadly, a full chat assistant. NotebookLM is for "what does our policy say about X?", Gemini is for everything else.
We're on Microsoft 365 — can we still access any of this?
Yes. Google for Nonprofits is independent of your email and file platform. A 501(c)(3) can apply for the $0/month Workspace license and use Gemini and NotebookLM without migrating from Microsoft 365. You just won't have the deep Drive integration.
Can I use a synthetic AI audience to test programs before piloting with real people?
Yes — with caveats. Tools include synthetic persona databases, council-style evaluations, and rubric-based scoring. Joshua recommended Daniel Miessler's open-source infrastructure as a starting point. The limitation: the AI knows what you're testing for and can give false positives. Real-world testing is still irreplaceable.
